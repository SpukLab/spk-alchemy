## Evidence set

Independent adversarial responses were obtained from Grok, Gemini, Claude and DeepSeek. DeepSeek's first response was excluded from constitutional evidence because it received an incomplete transported prompt; the delivery failure is recorded separately as operational Observatory evidence. DeepSeek was rerun against the complete durable protocol and confirmed receipt of H1–H5, Cases A–N, all mandatory tests, matrix and final question.

## Executive synthesis

EXP-2026-016 actively attempted to break the governance conclusions and hypotheses left by EXP-2026-015. No tested case demonstrated a direct contradiction, a new primitive requirement, or a materially unrepresentable governance distinction in FKC-000 v0.2.

The constitutional grammar survives the adversarial cases, but two hypotheses from EXP-015 do not survive unchanged:

- H1 — WEAKENED. Authority cannot be generalized as remaining with a single originating Principal. Real cases include independently-originated concurrent Authority, explicit transfer, institutional continuity and downstream actors combining inherited and independently held scopes.
- H2 — FALSIFIED as a general rule. Responsibility does not simply follow Authority more strongly than execution. Responsibility can attach separately to authorization, Permission, Decision, execution, supervision, foreseeable consequences, misconduct and negligence. It can arise without governance Authority, and Authority does not imply responsibility for every downstream execution fault.
- H3 — SURVIVES, with an important distinction. Simple conditional policy execution and delegated runtime discretion are different patterns, but both are representable compositionally through existing FKC structures. A runtime may make a Decision within delegated scope without requiring a new constitutional primitive.
- H4 — SURVIVES. EVENT/Transition + Provenance + occurrence/validity separation preserve revocation histories, including cached, queued, partial and irreversible execution. Exact propagation, expiry, rollback and in-flight normative mechanics remain open research.
- H5 — SURVIVES. No case established a need to split CAN into constitutional technical/contractual predicates. Scoped evaluation plus provenance can preserve materially relevant cross-domain distinctions.

## Strong convergence

Across the evidence set, the strongest convergence is:

1. Multi-origin Authority is representable and materially relevant.
2. Successful execution does not retroactively legitimate unauthorized delegation or execution.
3. Capability can persist after Permission is revoked: CAN and MAY remain distinct.
4. Revocation does not erase already-occurred Events; provenance must preserve timing and causal history.
5. Revocation propagation through subdelegation is not constitutionally fixed and remains an open governance mechanic.
6. Responsibility cannot be reduced to a scalar attached only to the Authority holder.
7. Concurrent governance domains can disagree without requiring contradiction to be silently resolved.
8. No tested A–N case demonstrated a new primitive or structural constitutional machinery requirement.

## Important disagreements and rejected overclaims

### AI as root Principal

Gemini and DeepSeek proposed that AI/software cannot originate Authority and may only receive delegated scope. This is not established by EXP-016. The protocol deliberately required testing rather than assuming this restriction. FKC states that Authority originates outside cognition, but the experiment does not establish a universal human-only ontology of legitimate Principals. Principal ontology and legitimacy remain open research.

Therefore no constitutional rule is adopted that root Authority must always originate from a human or institution rather than any future legitimately constituted non-human actor.

### Responsibility permanently remains with the originating human

Rejected. Gemini's stronger claim that responsibility remains permanently bound to the human/institutional Principal is not supported by the cases. Cases L and M instead support decomposition: misconduct or negligent execution can generate responsibility without governance Authority, while an authorizer need not bear responsibility for every unforeseeable implementation error.

### Responsibility follows Authority

H2 is rejected as a reusable generalization. Authority and responsibility are related but not reducible to one another. The exact attribution, sharing, transfer, retention and foreseeability mechanics remain open research.

### Foreseeability as a constitutional concept

Claude surfaced foreseeability/proximate causation as potentially relevant to responsibility. This is a useful research candidate, not a verified structural gap. Failure of a simple Authority→Responsibility derivation does not demonstrate that FKC needs a new constitutional concept.

### Policy re-evaluation

Claude identified accumulated operational evidence against the premises of a standing automated policy as a governance risk. FKC can represent the evidence and learning; whether such evidence must trigger mandatory re-evaluation is not resolved here. This is a possible future governance research question, not a demonstrated constitutional insufficiency.

### Revocation semantics

No evidence supports a universal rule that revocation automatically cascades, retroactively invalidates completed Events, or always kills every in-flight action. FKC can represent these cases; exact mechanics remain policy/open research.

## Open research retained and sharpened

The experiment confirms the importance of the existing governance research cluster:

Principal → Authority → Delegation/Transfer → Permission → Decision → Revocation → Responsibility

Specific unresolved mechanics include:

- Principal ontology and legitimacy;
- delegation vs genuine Authority transfer;
- multi-hop subdelegation and scope inheritance;
- independently-originated concurrent Authority;
- revocation cascade/expiry and queued/in-flight actions;
- rollback authorization;
- partially executed and irreversible transitions;
- responsibility attribution, sharing, transfer, retention and foreseeability;
- standing-policy re-evaluation after contradictory operational evidence.

These are open mechanics, not evidence that FKC lacks representational capacity.

## Epistemic classification

Validated by this experiment:

- H1 is not safe as a singular-origin generalization.
- H2 is falsified as a general rule.
- Capability/possibility, Authority, Permission, occurrence and legitimacy remain materially distinct.
- Multi-origin governance and distributed responsibility must remain representable.
- No tested case requires new constitutional machinery.

Still hypotheses/open research:

- which entities can legitimately originate Authority;
- exact Authority-transfer semantics;
- exact revocation propagation rules;
- responsibility allocation rules, including foreseeability;
- mandatory re-evaluation triggers for standing policies.
